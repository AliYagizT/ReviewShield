ReviewShield

A Multi-Signal Framework for Detecting Coordinated Fake Reviews in Turkish E-Commerce

Department of Computer Engineering · TED University · 2026–2027

About Approach Research Questions Team Documents

About the Project

Online product reviews strongly influence purchasing decisions, but their reliability can be damaged by coordinated reviewing activity, where groups of accounts publish misleading reviews to manipulate product ratings or harm competitors.

ReviewShield is a platform-independent software prototype that detects groups of accounts acting in coordination in Turkish product reviews. Instead of asking "Is this single review fake?", it asks "Is there a group of accounts behaving together?" Each review may look natural on its own; manipulation usually becomes visible only when review texts, posting times and account–product relationships are examined together.

The system accepts structured review data (CSV or JSON), assigns an explainable 0–100 suspicion score to candidate account groups, and presents the results in a web-based dashboard showing flagged groups, supporting evidence and graph relationships.

The suspicion score is a prioritization aid for human review, not proof that a review is fake. The project uses only authorized, anonymized datasets and does not scrape e-commerce platforms.

Approach

ReviewShield combines three complementary signals into a single, interpretable framework:

Turkish NLP

Sentence embeddings measure semantic similarity between reviews, catching near-duplicates, templates and paraphrases.

Temporal Analysis

Rolling-window and statistical methods detect unusual review bursts and near-simultaneous activity across accounts.

Graph Analysis

A weighted reviewer–product graph and community detection reveal accounts that repeatedly review the same products together.

Signals are fused into a transparent weighted score, and an ablation study compares text-only, time-only, graph-only and combined configurations. Planned stack: Python, Sentence Transformers, NetworkX, FastAPI and React.

Research Questions

  • Does combining text, temporal and graph signals improve detection of coordinated groups compared with individual signals?
  • How well does the approach recover controlled groups under different levels of textual similarity and temporal coordination?
  • Which signals contribute to a flagged result, and what common legitimate behaviors generate false alarms?
